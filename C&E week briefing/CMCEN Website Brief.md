# Communications and Electronics Week 2026

## CMCEN Website Brief — 30 Minutes

### Purpose

Present the new CMCEN website as a modernization of the CMCEN/RCMCE online
presence: retaining its community purpose while making information easier to
find, use, manage, and sustain.

The presentation should respect the value of the legacy site and focus on the
improvements delivered by the new platform. Confirm any legacy-specific claims
against screenshots or records before presenting them as fact.

## Run of Show

| Time | Section | Key message |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0–3 min | Welcome and purpose | Why the new CMCEN website was needed: a modern, community-focused digital home for the C&E family. |
| 3–7 min | The legacy site: what we learned | Recognize the legacy CMCEN/RCMCE site's contribution, then explain the need for clearer navigation, easier content stewardship, improved discovery, and greater self-service. Use legacy screenshots if available. |
| 7–11 min | What changed at a glance | A refreshed visual identity, responsive design, clearer information architecture, bilingual experience, and a platform designed to evolve. |
| 11–18 min | Member-facing improvements | Demonstrate news and stories, events, retirement messages, last-post notices, professional recognition, search, and mobile-friendly navigation. Emphasize faster access to community information. |
| 18–23 min | Community contribution and engagement | Show how members or designated contributors can submit content and event information, with review and publishing workflows. Include RSVP/event support when it is within the demonstrated release. |
| 23–27 min | Behind the scenes: stewardship | Explain the administrator workspace: role-based access, content and media management, translation support, audit logging, and optional analytics. The message: less reliance on one technical custodian and stronger accountability. |
| 27–29 min | Security, accessibility, and sustainability | Cover responsible access controls, auditability, responsive layouts, bilingual support, and a maintainable platform that can accommodate future improvements. |
| 29–30 min | Close and call to action | Invite attendees to visit the site, provide feedback, contribute stories or events, and identify content that should be migrated or improved next. |

## Suggested Presenter Split

- **Presenter 1:** Context; legacy-to-new comparison; member-facing walkthrough.
- **Presenter 2:** Platform stewardship; security and governance; roadmap and call to action.
- **Both presenters:** Live demonstration and questions.

## Legacy-to-New Comparison

Use this as a discussion aid, adapting the legacy column to verified examples
from the former site.

| Legacy experience | New CMCEN website |
| --- | --- |
| Information could be difficult to discover | Site-wide search and clearer navigation |
| Primarily a static web presence | Active news, events, recognition, and community notices |
| Content updates could depend on technical intervention | Defined contributor, reviewer, and administrator workflows |
| Limited visibility into administrative changes | Role-based access and audit logging |
| Older layouts were less suited to phones | Responsive experience across desktop and mobile |
| Maintaining English and French content was more difficult | Built-in bilingual content and translation management |
| Harder to extend safely | Modern application foundation with documented configuration and APIs |

## Demonstration Checklist

1. Start at the home page and show navigation on a desktop or mobile-sized view.
2. Search for a community item.
3. Open a news story, event, retirement message, or recognition item.
4. Show the member/contributor journey for submitting content, if appropriate for the audience.
5. Show the administrator workspace, focusing on permissions, review workflows, or the audit log rather than sensitive data.

## Key Takeaways

1. The new site is more than a visual refresh; it is a sustainable community platform.
2. It makes CMCEN information and stories easier for members to discover and engage with.
3. It gives authorized stewards clearer tools and accountability for managing content.
4. It provides a foundation for future community needs without requiring another full rebuild.

## Suggested Closing

> This is not simply a visual refresh. It is a sustainable community platform
> that lets CMCEN share its stories, recognize its people, and keep members
> connected.

## Preparation Notes

- Capture a few verified legacy-site screenshots for the comparison section.
- Prepare sample content that contains no personal, operationally sensitive, or restricted information.
- Decide whether the session will include a live site demonstration, recorded walkthrough, or both.
- Reserve the final minute for a clear feedback channel and next steps.

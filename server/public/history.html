History & heritage

History

The Communications and Electronics Branch carries forward a long tradition of Canadian military communications.

Founding services

The Communications and Electronics Branch is responsible for communications and electronics matters in the Canadian Armed Forces. Its founding services include the Royal Canadian Corps of Signals, Royal Canadian Air Force Telecommunications Branch, Royal Canadian Navy Communications Research Branch, and elements of the Royal Canadian Electrical and Mechanical Engineers.

Origins of Canadian signals

Major Matthew Wallace Bruce Carruthers recognized the importance of reliable battlefield communications after the South African War. His proposal for a specialized Signals Corps was accepted, and General Order 167 authorized the Canadian Signal Corps (Militia) on 24 October 1903—the first independent organized Signal Corps in the British Empire.

From the First World War onward, Canadian communicators adapted rapidly to new technologies, including wireless, cable, dispatch riders, visual signalling, radio navigation, radar, and satellite communications.

A unified C&E Branch

In 1968, military communicators from the Navy, Army, and Air Force came together under the Canadian Forces Reorganization Act. Training continued at the Canadian Forces School of Communications and Electronics in Kingston, Ontario.

Today, military communicators and civilian Defence Team members support Canadian Armed Forces operations in Canada and abroad. They continue to live the motto Velox, Versutus, Vigilans—Swift, Accurate, and Watchful.
